# Cover letter draft — Theoretical Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Interaction thresholds and state separation under fragmentation: a theorem-guided finite-model framework,”** for publication in *Theoretical Ecology*.

Fragmentation, ecological function, trait persistence and genetic variation are often discussed as if they tracked one common deterioration axis. Our manuscript asks a narrower theoretical question: when an interaction-dependent eco-genetic system is fragmented, which conclusions follow exactly from the declared maps, and which biological states can separate once finite recruitment and inheritance are introduced?

The paper combines two exact analytical layers with a preregistered finite-model test. For a canonical positive-feedback interaction map we derive the exact condition for strict bistability and distinguish branch-dependent potential trait viability from realised occupancy. For an arbitrary destination-by-source row-stochastic allele-frequency mixing matrix we give common-floor and focal-rescue bounds while explicitly separating composition mixing from demographic or trait rescue. We then project the same prepared finite source state across a fixed-area fragmentation gradient. Across 1,037 sources that support every tested patch count, potential high-trait viability changes from present in every one-patch outcome to absent at every tested subdivision, while realised high-trait occupancy persists in approximately 99.6–100% of trajectories at the 30-generation endpoint. Interaction and local effective size continue to decline with subdivision, whereas realised high-trait mass shows an initial loss followed by partial recovery.

The ecological conclusion is therefore not that fragmentation produces one universal monotone response. It is that **potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, effective-size state and genetic summaries are distinct biological objects whose relevance depends on the question being asked**. The manuscript also makes its evidential hierarchy explicit: mathematical theorems, closure-conditional implications and finite stochastic evidence are not allowed to substitute for one another.

We believe this is a strong fit for *Theoretical Ecology* because the contribution uses mathematical and computational theory to answer an ecological question while keeping the biological interpretation and the limits of each model layer explicit. The manuscript is not a parameter-search report and does not claim that its canonical threshold or finite fragmentation responses are universal. Predictive warning validity and downstream forecast-state sufficiency are intentionally reserved for separately versioned companion work and are not used to enlarge the present claim.

All equations, finite-model code, protocol locks and evidence ledgers are version controlled.
