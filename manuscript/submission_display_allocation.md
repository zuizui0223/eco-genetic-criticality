# EGC submission display allocation

This is the active display contract for the standalone EGC manuscript. It compresses the earlier exploratory `figure_table_plan.md` without changing any theorem, finite result, denominator, or claim ceiling.

## Main displays — exactly five items

### Figure 1 — From fragmentation to distinct biological states

**Role:** orient the reader before equations.

Show the hypothesis/evidence architecture:

```text
patch size
  -> interaction state q
  -> potential high-trait viability
  -> realised occupancy / local effective size
  -> allele persistence and genetic summaries
```

Annotate each arrow by evidential status: exact theorem, declared conditional implication, or finite Type S closure. The figure must make clear that the chain is a research architecture, not one theorem.

### Figure 2 — Exact analytical boundaries

Two panels.

**A. Interaction feedback.** Show the canonical fixed-point geometry and strict bistability condition for the declared sigmoid map, including the `K=4` boundary and the low/stable, middle/unstable and high/stable branches where applicable.

**B. Network mixing.** Show the row-stochastic allele-frequency update and the common-floor/focal-rescue bounds. State explicitly that the operator moves composition, not individuals, abundance, pollen, seeds or pollinators.

This figure owns the exact Type T material. It must not contain finite fragmentation outcomes.

### Figure 3 — Fixed-source fragmentation gradient separates realised responses

Combine the preregistered design with the three realised response curves.

- inset: one prepared full source -> 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 equal isolated patches;
- annotate the common supported denominator: 1,037 prepared sources across every tested patch count;
- panel A: retained interaction;
- panel B: retained local effective size;
- panel C: retained realised high-trait mass.

The visual point is that interaction and effective size continue downward while realised high-trait mass shows an initial loss followed by partial recovery. Do not imply a universal monotone fragmentation law.

### Figure 4 — Potential viability and realised occupancy are different states

Display the sharp contrast that motivates the paper's state-separation conclusion:

- one patch: potential high-trait viability present in 1,037/1,037 supported outcomes;
- every tested subdivision: potential viability absent in 1,037/1,037;
- generation-30 realised high-trait occupancy nevertheless persists in approximately 99.6–100% of supported trajectories.

Caption must say this is finite Type S state-separation under the declared closure, not a universal biological lag law.

### Table 1 — Evidence status and state non-equivalences

Merge the useful material from the former proposed Tables 1 and 2.

Columns:

- object/claim;
- mathematical or biological definition;
- evidence label (`T`, `C`, `S`, or historical/non-headline where applicable);
- what it is not equivalent to;
- manuscript section / source artifact.

Rows should cover at minimum interaction state, potential viability, realised occupancy, local effective size, allele-frequency persistence, `H_alpha`, `H_gamma`, differentiation where relevant, canonical bistability and network-mixing bounds.

## Explicit exclusions from the main paper

Do not add:

- a main warning figure;
- a “best genetic warning” metric;
- EGWE cross-layer alignment / 0.2543 state-sufficiency evidence;
- EGWE propagation-horizon results;
- EGWEE natural-data examples;
- new parameter sweeps created only to make an extra display.

The five-item allocation is intended to make the theorem -> finite closure -> state-separation story readable without importing downstream series claims.
