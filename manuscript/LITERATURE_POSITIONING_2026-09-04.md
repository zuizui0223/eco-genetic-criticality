# EGC literature and novelty positioning — 2026-09-04

## Purpose

This document closes the literature-placement gap in the standalone EGC manuscript without changing any theorem, finite closure, frozen simulation result, or claim ceiling.

The standalone paper is **not** a warning paper and is **not** the state-sufficiency paper. Its publication-level contribution is:

> **A theorem-guided finite framework in which fragmentation acts on interaction support but potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction, effective size and genetic state are explicitly non-interchangeable biological objects.**

The literature pass should make the paper legible as a contribution to fragmentation ecology, eco-evolutionary dynamics and ecological function, while preserving the theorem/closure/finite-evidence taxonomy.

## 1. Introduction placement

### 1.1 Opening problem — ecological loss is not one variable

Recommended insertion after the first Introduction paragraph:

> Ecological deterioration is often reported through population size, species persistence, interaction loss, functional performance or genetic diversity, but these quantities need not fail together. Strongly interacting species can cease to deliver ecological function well before numerical extinction, and interaction loss itself can precede species loss (Soulé et al. 2005; McConkey & Drake 2006; Valiente-Banuet et al. 2015). Genetic diversity is likewise ecologically consequential without being a context-free surrogate for realised ecological function (Hughes et al. 2008). This motivates treating interaction support, potential viability, realised occupancy, demographic state and genetic summaries as distinct biological objects rather than interchangeable readouts of one latent deterioration axis.

Why it belongs here:

- connects the manuscript's state-separation result to empirical ecology;
- does not claim that any cited study implements the EGC closure;
- makes `state separation` biologically necessary before the mathematical machinery begins.

### 1.2 Fragmentation is multidimensional, not a single scalar treatment

Recommended insertion after the causal-chain paragraph:

> Fragmentation further complicates this separation because habitat amount, spatial configuration, landscape quality, movement and interaction opportunities need not vary in the same direction. Eco-evolutionary theory has long emphasized that dispersal, demography and selection interact in fragmented landscapes (Legrand et al. 2017), while recent syntheses argue explicitly for functional rather than purely structural interpretations of fragmentation (Benitez et al. 2025). Empirical network work also shows that habitat loss and fragmentation per se can have different consequences for interaction structure (Gama et al. 2025), and that landscape quality can condition responses to loss and fragmentation (Fletcher et al. 2026). The present model therefore treats patchwise interaction support and movement operators as declared mechanisms rather than assuming that one fragmentation label uniquely determines ecological or genetic state.

Why it belongs here:

- prevents the paper from being read as `more patches = universally worse`;
- supports the fixed-area/full-state-transfer counterfactual;
- protects against overclaiming the finite fragmentation gradient as a universal landscape law.

### 1.3 Interaction structure can be functionally relevant

Optional short insertion before the contribution paragraph:

> The emphasis on interaction state is also consistent with evidence that ecological function and persistence can depend on interaction architecture and partner functional composition rather than species counts alone (Hiraiwa & Ushimaru 2024; Domínguez-Garcia et al. 2024). EGC does not model those empirical systems directly; they motivate retaining interaction support as a distinct state coordinate instead of subsuming it into abundance.

### 1.4 Critical-transition literature — use narrowly

Scheffer et al. (2009) may be cited once to situate the positive-feedback/bistability language, but EGC should not drift into the warning literature.

Recommended wording:

> Positive feedback and alternative stable states are central motifs in critical-transition theory (Scheffer et al. 2009). Here, however, the analytical result is deliberately narrower: bistability is proved only for the declared one-dimensional sigmoid map, and no generic early-warning property follows from that geometry.

Do **not** add the warning-validity literature here. That belongs to EGWE warning.

## 2. Discussion placement

### 2.1 Functional loss before numerical disappearance

Recommended insertion after the first Discussion distinction:

> The resulting separation between potential support and realised occupancy has a natural ecological interpretation. Functional contributions can disappear before population disappearance, as illustrated by interaction-dependent seed dispersal and other strongly interacting species (Soulé et al. 2005; McConkey & Drake 2006; Valiente-Banuet et al. 2015). The EGC result is not an empirical estimate of that lag. It supplies a finite closure in which the analogous logical separation can be made explicit: a trait can remain realised for a finite interval after the declared interaction environment no longer supports its potential viability.

### 2.2 Migration statement boundary

Recommended insertion after the second Discussion distinction:

> This operator-specific caution is especially important in fragmented landscapes, where dispersal can couple demography, genetic composition and selection in different ways (Legrand et al. 2017). Plant genetic responses to habitat fragmentation are empirically heterogeneous rather than governed by one universal structural-fragmentation effect (Miguel-Peñaloza et al. 2023). The row-stochastic allele-frequency bounds in EGC should therefore be read as exact statements about the declared composition operator, not as a general theorem that connectivity is beneficial or harmful.

### 2.3 State-separation novelty paragraph

Replace the current sentence

> `Which state is sufficient therefore depends on the scientific target.`

with:

> **Which biological state is relevant therefore depends on the scientific question; forecast sufficiency is a separate problem developed in the extension programme.**

Then add:

> This distinction is the manuscript's main state-level contribution. Genetic diversity can affect ecological processes (Hughes et al. 2008), and fragmented-landscape dynamics can involve coupled demographic and evolutionary feedbacks (Legrand et al. 2017; Govaert et al. 2019), but those observations do not license a single summary to stand in for interaction support, potential viability, realised occupancy, effective size and allele persistence. The fragmentation gradient provides a controlled finite counterexample to that compression under one declared closure.

This wording deliberately hands **forecast sufficiency** to EGWE state validity.

## 3. Novelty firewall

### Supported novelty

1. **Evidence-status discipline.** Exact theorem, closure-conditional statement and finite Type S result are kept distinct in one eco-genetic fragmentation programme.
2. **State-separation construction.** Potential viability, realised occupancy, interaction support, local effective size, genetic diversity and allele persistence are not treated as one latent condition.
3. **Full-state-transfer fragmentation counterfactual.** The finite comparison asks what changes when the same prepared state is projected into equal isolated fragments, rather than reconstructing a new low-dimensional source summary.
4. **Different fragmentation response shapes.** In the frozen gradient, potential viability changes at the first subdivision, realised occupancy persists over the finite endpoint, interaction/Ne continue downward and realised high-trait mass partially recovers.

### Not novel / do not claim

- positive feedback or alternative stable states in ecology;
- the general idea that ecological function can be lost before numerical extinction;
- the general idea that fragmentation has ecological and genetic effects;
- the general idea that genetic diversity is ecologically important;
- the general idea that dispersal can rescue or erode variation;
- state sufficiency for forecasting;
- predictive genetic early warning;
- a universal fragmentation dose-response;
- a universal claim that more patches are worse;
- a natural-system estimate of the model's lag or threshold.
